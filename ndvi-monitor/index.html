How This Works

What are the indices?

NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) measures plant health using near-infrared and red bands. Values range from -1 to 1:

  • Green > 0.6 — Dense, healthy vegetation
  • Yellow 0.3–0.6 — Moderate or sparse vegetation
  • Red < 0.3 — Bare soil, water, or stressed crops

NDWI detects surface water (green & NIR bands). LSWI (Land Surface Water Index, NIR & SWIR) is more sensitive to moisture in canopy and soil — useful for spotting transplanting and flooded paddies. Toggle between all three with the segmented buttons in the bottom panel.

How to use this app

  1. Sign in — Click "Sign in with Google" to authenticate with Earth Engine.
  2. Drag the slider — Move the time slider at the bottom to see NDVI/NDWI/LSWI change month by month.
  3. Click anywhere on the map to see a detailed trend chart, stress alerts, and recent rainfall context.
  4. Draw a field — Use the polygon/rectangle tools to outline a rice paddy, then name it and optionally set a planting date.
  5. Check the dashboard — Click ☰ to see your saved fields with live health status, growth stage, and area.
  6. Compare months — Toggle "Compare" to view two months side-by-side with synced maps.
  7. Export — Click "Export" to download the trend chart as PNG or a full PDF report with chart, field info, and health summary.

Growth stages

If you set a planting date for a field, the app compares the current NDVI against expected ranges for each rice growth stage:

  • Germination (Days 1–10) — Bare soil / shallow water: NDVI 0.05–0.15
  • Seedling (Days 11–25) — Sparse green: NDVI 0.15–0.30
  • Vegetative (Days 26–55) — Rapid growth: NDVI 0.30–0.55
  • Reproductive (Days 56–90) — Peak biomass: NDVI 0.55–0.75
  • Maturation (Days 91–110) — Senescing: NDVI 0.35–0.55
  • Harvest (Days 111+) — Dry-down: NDVI 0.10–0.30

Data source

Satellite imagery from Sentinel-2 (ESA), processed by Google Earth Engine. Rainfall data from CHIRPS (UCSB/USGS). Area of interest: rice-growing region near Battambang, Cambodia.
